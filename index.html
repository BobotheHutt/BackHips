Hip Joint Basics

The hip joint works like a heavy-duty ball-and-socket assembly designed to carry your upper body weight. When the smooth cushioning tissue inside the joint wears thin over time, the bones can rub closer together. This extra friction causes the surrounding muscles to tighten up automatically to try and shield the joint.

🔧 Standard Hip Routines

Glute Bridges
10 Reps / 3 Second Hold

Lie on your back with knees bent. Push firmly through your heels to raise your hips until your body forms a straight line from shoulders to knees. Squeeze the muscles to build stability around the joints.

Seated Chair Stretch
3 Sets / 30 Second Hold

Sit upright in a solid chair. Cross one ankle flat over the opposite knee to make a "figure 4". Keeping your back straight, lean your upper body slightly forward until you feel a comfortable pull in the outer hip muscle.

Lower Back Basics

The lower spine is supported by small bone blocks separated by rubbery, fluid-filled shock absorbers called discs. When these spaces experience heavy, constant pressure from sitting or standing out of alignment, it can squeeze the nearby nerve roots that travel from your spine down into your legs.

🔧 Standard Back Routines

Cat-Cow Mobility Drill
10 Movements

Get on your hands and knees. Slowly let your stomach sink down toward the floor while looking up slightly. Then, gently round your back up toward the ceiling like an arching cat while tucking your chin. Keep it fluid and smooth.

Bird-Dog Core Stability
12 Alternating Reps

On hands and knees, tighten your midsection. Slowly raise and straighten your right arm straight ahead while pushing your left leg straight back behind you. Hold for 2 seconds, drop down, and switch sides to brace the core.
