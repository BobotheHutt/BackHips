1. Hip Anatomy & Physiology

The hip is a robust ball-and-socket joint designed to bear body weight and handle wide ranges of movement. Over time, the protective cartilage cushioning the femoral head can wear down, leading to increased friction, mild localized inflammation, or structural mechanical vulnerabilities.

Supporting muscles—such as the gluteus medius, tensor fasciae latae, and deep hip rotators—often tighten up defensively to stabilize a vulnerable joint. Keeping these surrounding muscle groups balanced and long is vital to alleviating excessive compression inside the hip capsule itself.

2. Physical Therapy & Management

Glute Bridges

Lying on your back with knees bent, press down firmly through your heels to raise your hips until your body forms a straight line from your shoulders to your knees. Hold for 3 seconds, then lower smoothly. Complete 10 repetitions to wake up the posterior stabilizing muscles.

Seated Figure-4 Stretch

Sit upright on a stable chair. Cross your right ankle over your left knee. Keeping your chest high and back tall, gently hinge forward from the hips until you feel a deep, comfortable stretch across the outer hip and glute. Hold for 20-30 seconds per side.
